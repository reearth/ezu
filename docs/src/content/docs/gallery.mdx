Every image here comes from an example style in the repository, rendered with
`ezu tile` or `ezu bbox`. The style JSON is the whole recipe — copy it, change a
colour, render again.

## pencil-sketch

Ten wave-then-line stroke layers, factored into one user-defined function, plus a
`water-hatch` function for the sea. This is the front-page render.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-pencil-sketch.webp" alt="A pencil-sketch render of central Tokyo" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Central Tokyo at z13, three tiles across.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/pencil-sketch.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out pencil.png
```

## watercolor

Scatter-dab fills through the `hokusai` brush engine, anchored in world
coordinates so the wash continues across tile edges. Parametric: `paper` and
`softness` are declared knobs.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-watercolor.webp" alt="A watercolour render of central Tokyo" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>The same ground in `watercolor.json`, at its declared defaults.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/watercolor.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out water.png

# …or with the knobs turned
ezu tile --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/watercolor.json \
  --tile 13/7276/3225 --param 'paper=#ffe0f0' --param softness=2 --out water.png
```

Style: [`watercolor.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/watercolor.json)

## photo-pop

Not a vector style at all: GSI aerial photography pulled in as a `raster` source,
saturation pushed up with `hsl`, then `posterize` to twelve steps per channel.
Four nodes.

  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-photo-pop.webp" alt="A high-contrast posterized render of central Tokyo" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Colour and palette ops rather than brushes — the same data, pushed hard.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/photo-pop.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out pop.png
```

## atlas

A plain labelled basemap, written by hand rather than translated. Every label
layer lowers to a `text-labels` node, all three meet in one `label-placement`, and
a `text-draw` per layer paints the winners — so a city name can knock out the road
label underneath it, and the decision is the same from whichever tile you approach
it. Roads are `stroke` pairs whose widths are `interpolate` expressions, so one
recipe covers every zoom.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-atlas.webp" alt="A labelled reference map of central Tokyo" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Central Tokyo at z13. The labels are placed per tile, yet read as one sheet — that is the shared collision index working across borders.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/atlas.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out atlas.png
```

Style: [`atlas.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/atlas.json)
· Cookbook: [labels and icons](/ezu/cookbook/labels-and-icons/)
· Concepts: [padding and neighbours](/ezu/concepts/padding-and-neighbours/)

## risograph

Two drums and a black pass. Area fills print through a halftone screen — a
`point-grid` of `circles` used as a `blend` mask — while linework prints solid, the
way it does on a real duplicator. Each drum's features are `transform`ed a couple
of pixels out of register, and a closing `quantize` snaps the whole sheet onto the
ink palette plus its overprints, so nothing survives that the press could not mix.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-risograph.webp" alt="A two-colour risograph print of central Tokyo" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Fluorescent pink over blue on cream stock. The dots are a masked point grid, not a texture image.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/risograph.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out riso.png

# coarser screen, fatter dots
ezu tile --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/risograph.json \
  --tile 13/7276/3225 --param screen-px=9 --param dot-px=3 --out riso.png
```

Style: [`risograph.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/risograph.json)
· Cookbook: [halftone and dither](/ezu/cookbook/halftone-and-dither/)

## topo

The same DEM tiles as `hillshade`, read three ways: `contour` pulls isolines out of
the elevation field as real vector features (stroked twice, thin for the interval
and heavy for every fifth index line), `hillshade` shades the relief, and `slope`
feeds a `levels` → `color-to-alpha` chain that darkens only the cliff faces. The
index interval is a param, so `interval-m` and `index-every` re-cut the contours
without touching the recipe.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-topo.webp" alt="A topographic map of the Japanese Northern Alps" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>The Northern Alps around Kamikōchi at z13 — 100 m contours, 500 m index lines, no vector contour source anywhere.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/topo.json \
  --bbox 137.60,36.20,137.72,36.28 --zoom 13 --out topo.png

# 200 m contours instead
ezu tile --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/topo.json \
  --tile 13/7253/3229 --param interval-m=200 --out topo.png
```

Style: [`topo.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/topo.json)
· Cookbook: [hillshade and hypsometric tint](/ezu/cookbook/hillshade-and-hypsometric/)

## poi-field

Analysis rather than cartography: `density` turns the POI layer into a
kernel-density `ScalarField`, `color-ramp` colours it through a MapLibre
`heatmap-color` expression (`ramp-expr`), and the same field goes into `contour` a
second time to draw isolines over the heat — including a single heavier line at the
0.75 level. Kernel radius and isoline interval are params.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-poi-field.webp" alt="A heatmap of points of interest across central Tokyo with isolines" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Where the shops are, at z13. Heat and isolines are two readings of one field node.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/poi-field.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out field.png

# wider kernel, coarser isolines
ezu tile --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/poi-field.json \
  --tile 13/7276/3225 --param radius-px=48 --param step=0.2 --out field.png
```

Style: [`poi-field.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/poi-field.json)
· Concepts: [ports and types](/ezu/concepts/ports-and-types/)

## stained-glass

Geometry ops as the whole design. `voronoi-fracture` breaks the land, water, and
park polygons into panes around seed points — the POI layer where it has points, a
`point-grid` where it does not — and `boundary` + `stroke` turns every pane edge
into leading. Pane colour comes from a world-anchored `worley` field mapped through
an HCL ramp and clipped to the panes, so neighbouring glass differs without any
per-cell attribute to key on.

<figure class="page-fig">
  <img src="/ezu/figures/gallery-stained-glass.webp" alt="Central Tokyo rendered as a stained-glass window" width="768" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
  <figcaption>Panes seeded from POIs, leading from their boundaries, colour from a noise field.</figcaption>
</figure>

```sh
ezu bbox --style crates/ezu/examples/styles/stained-glass.json \
  --bbox 139.72,35.66,139.80,35.72 --zoom 13 --out glass.png
```

Style: [`stained-glass.json`](https://github.com/reearth/ezu/blob/main/crates/ezu/examples/styles/stained-glass.json)
· Nodes: [geometry](/ezu/style/nodes/geometry/)

:::caution[Voronoi cells and tile seams]
Panes are built from the seeds visible in one tile plus its `pad`, so a cell that
would extend past that window can differ between neighbouring tiles. It is fine for
a single render or a print, and visible if you pan a seamless map — raise `pad`, or
seed from a world-anchored `point-grid` alone, if you need the seams to agree.
:::

## A translated MapLibre basemap

Not painterly at all: a Protomaps basemap MapLibre style, converted with
`ezu translate` and rendered by ezu, labels included. This is the render
`ezu-compare` pixel-diffs against maplibre-gl-js.

```sh
ezu translate https://api.protomaps.com/styles/v5/light/en.json --out recipe.json
ezu tile --style recipe.json --tile 14/14550/6452 --out basemap.png
```

Guide: [from a MapLibre style](/ezu/guides/from-maplibre/)

:::note[Brushes and licensing]
The painterly example styles reference CC0 brushes by David Revoy through a
relative `file:` path, so render them from a checkout of the repository. Nothing
is bundled into the library — a style names every brush it uses.
:::
